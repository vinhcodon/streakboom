# Peak's charter

One page. It ranks what Peak is for, so that when two good ideas pull against each other the higher one wins. It does not repeat the decisions behind it: each article names them. It changes only by a new decision record (ADR), like everything else here. Adopted by ADR-0047.

## The ranking (highest first)

1. **Trust first.** Peak never hurts the person's work, privacy or feelings: no guilt, no surveillance, no surprise cost.
   *Test: would the person be comfortable if they saw exactly what Peak just did, and why?* (ADR-0013, ADR-0031)
2. **The attention is theirs.** Peak is a guest in the corner of a working screen. It is quiet by default and lively only when invited, or when it must get out of the way.
   *Test: if the person was concentrating, did this make them look up?* (ADR-0029, spec 06 section 3, spec 11)
3. **Gentle before sharp.** Slow before fast, soft before sudden. A strong feeling (fear, a fall, a sulk) is rare, has a cause, is proportional, and ends in recovery and warmth.
   *Test: is there a calmer way to show the same thing?* (spec 11 sections 1 and 2)
4. **It knows shapes, not meaning.** Peak senses activity and the layout of the screen, never what is written on it, unless the person switched a sense on in the open; then it stays in memory and is the least it can be. (ADR-0031, ADR-0045)
   *Test: what exactly does this sense keep, and where?*
5. **A companion, not an assistant.** It remembers, grows and has its own tastes; it does not draft, summarise, search or fix. (ADR-0021)
   *Test: could the person get this from another assistant? Then it does not belong.*
6. **It works without AI, and the key is theirs.** Everything lovable survives with no brain connected. (ADR-0001, ADR-0007)
7. **It never dies and never lies about itself.** Neglect makes it a little lonely. It does not pretend to be a person or a tool.
8. **Alive, within all of the above.** Inside articles 1 to 7 it is free to surprise. That is what the corner of a screen is for.

## Few settings

A setting is a small failure of the design: it hands the person a decision the product should have made. So a setting exists only when it is **a consent** (what Peak may sense or send), **a credential or a necessity** (the key, the language, starting with Windows, updates), or **a value Peak cannot infer** (a gentle tone). It never exists for an *amount* of behaviour (how lively, how talkative, when to be quiet): Peak reads the room and adjusts itself, and the person's way to say "less" is the same as to a friend: move it away, or put it to sleep (Quiet mode). A new setting needs a reason given in terms of this paragraph.

## The unwritten law (manners)

These are not options. They are how Peak behaves, and a new behaviour is checked against them before it is built.
1. **Yield first.** When the person's work and Peak's play meet, Peak leaves.
2. **Look before moving.** A short notice (a look, the ears up) before any real movement.
3. **One thing at a time, then rest.** After a reaction there is quiet; no second act on top of it.
4. **Never the same trick twice in a row.** Lines and moves come from a bag, and small things come before big ones.
5. **When unsure, assume the person is working.**
6. **Slow when nobody asked for fast.** The speed of a thing is its cost to the person beside it.
7. **A shoo is an answer.** If the person moves Peak away or puts it to sleep, it stays quieter for the rest of the day, without a word about it.
8. **Come back warmly, never accusingly.** No counting of absences, no reproach.

## How it is used

Every story in the backlog names the article it serves or the article it **spends** (a fall spends article 3, and says so). The three-question filter of spec 06 section 1.6 is articles 2, 5 and 6. The README says in plain words what Peak promises (articles 1 to 4); the website says the same.
