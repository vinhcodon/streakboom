# ADR-0048: A short, gentle website: a short home, details behind links, the charter's promises, one round of the tour

- **Status:** accepted (decisions answered by the author on 2026-11-10); **nothing is built yet**
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Decided by:** Vinh (author), with Claude (the website session), answering the request in `docs/handoff/website-restructure-request.md`

## Context
The author said the site had become long-winded, and Peak now has a charter (`docs/charter.md`, ADR-0047). The measurement of 2026-11-10 (plan `website-seo-geo.md` section 14): the English home page is 2,862 words (Vietnamese 3,198; Japanese about 8,600 characters) in nine blocks, with ten FAQ answers of 19 to 137 words; the how-it-works page 659 words and the privacy page 623; `llms-full.txt` 3,037. The same fact is said up to nine times (works without AI: 9 places; free and MIT: 7; the free tiers: 7; the home page's "at a glance", "not", privacy block, feature cards, FAQ and the how-it-works page overlap). A first-time visitor needs the hero, a few promises, the install steps and a handful of answers; everything else is for people who want the detail.

## Decision
1. **Structure S1, a short home.** The home page is about **600 words** in English (Vietnamese about 12% more, Japanese about 3 characters per English word): the hero, **four short promises**, four one-line feature lines, the install in three steps, **five FAQ answers of one or two sentences**, and only the newest update with a link to the changelog. The detail moves behind links: the **how-it-works page** (about 900 words: the features in detail, the gestures table, the rest of the FAQ) and the **privacy page** (about 500 words: exactly what Peak senses and keeps, and where). The "at a glance", "not" and privacy blocks of the home page are merged into the promises and the FAQ; a fact is said in one place and the others link to it. `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt` and the structured data stay generated from the same strings, so they shrink with them.
2. **The charter on the site, in a distilled form.** At most five promises in Peak's voice, in the three languages, on the home page and as the opening of the privacy page, with one line linking to `docs/charter.md` on GitHub as the proof. No separate charter page; the "few settings" article and the manners are not published (the tour shows the manners). Only what the released app does is promised.
3. **Four promises now, the fifth later.** Today: it never reads what is on your screen or what you type (and the privacy page says exactly what it does notice); it never guilts you and never dies; it works without AI and your key is yours; it is a companion, not an assistant. The promise "it keeps quiet while you work" is **added only after CH-2 and CH-3 are released** (the audit of spec 11 found seven faults against article 2). Until then the site says only what is already true, in the FAQ: quiet in fullscreen and presentation, makes way when you work next to it. The opt-in screen picture (ADR-0045) is not mentioned until it exists.
4. **The hero tour plays one round, then stands still** (`LOOPS` from 2 to 1; about 45 seconds). It still pauses on hover, focus, when hidden or off screen, has the Pause button, and does not move by itself with `prefers-reduced-motion`; no pop-ups, nothing that interrupts the reading.
5. **The voice** is Peak's: warm, short, a little playful, never loud, no jargon, no claim we cannot back. Examples for the headline, one feature and one FAQ answer are in plan section 14. The Vietnamese and Japanese rewrites are written by the website session and have no native reader; they stay marked as such in the verification list.
6. **What does not change** (ADR-0034): per-language URLs, the crawlable changelog, structured data, `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt`, the sitemap, `facts.mjs` as the source of truth, the build checks. The build checks that depend on text now on the home page (the nine feature titles, `softwareRequirements`, the README numbers, the "under its feet" wording) are moved to the page that carries the text or changed to link-aware checks, and the FAQ in the structured data stays exactly the visible one on every page that has an FAQ. Only released behaviour (0.3.1) is described; the author's name is Vinh Codon.

## Alternatives considered
S2, a medium home of about 1,100 words that keeps six feature cards and a short gestures table (less change, still twice as long); S3, trimming in place to about 1,800 words (least risk for search, but still one long page); the charter as a link in the footer only, as a full page, or not at all; five promises with the fifth limited to what is true, or waiting for CH-2 and CH-3 before any promise; leaving the tour at two rounds, or standing still until the visitor touches it.

## Consequences
- Less text to keep true in three languages, and a single home for each fact; the promises are a public commitment, so every promise needs a check or a README line behind it (the build will check that each promise key has a matching sentence in the README or the charter and that the link to the charter resolves).
- Search and answer engines: the home page carries fewer words, but the facts stay easy to extract (short FAQ answers, the structured data, `llms.txt`); the detail is crawlable on the how-it-works and privacy pages. The FAQPage structured data must match the visible FAQ of each page that has one.
- Not verified: how search or AI answers react to a shorter home page (measure again with the fixed question set after the crawlers have visited); that the shortened Vietnamese and Japanese are good; the app's behaviour against article 2 until E15.
