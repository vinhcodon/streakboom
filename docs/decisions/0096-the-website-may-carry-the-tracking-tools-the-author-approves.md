# ADR-0096: The website may carry the measurement and tracking tools the author approves; the app's privacy contract is unchanged

- **Status:** accepted (Vinh, 2026-11-14: the website promotes a product and should carry the usual tools, protected by Cloudflare; he chose to approve the tools one by one and to have a consent notice for visitors outside Vietnam and Japan); **built for the first tool (Cloudflare Web Analytics, switched off until its token is given)**
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Decided by:** Vinh (author)
- **Supersedes** D7 of ADR-0034 ("no analytics script on the site"), item 5 of ADR-0051 and section 6 of `docs/handoff/website-stickers.md` for the website ("the app's rule holds on the site"); **builds on** ADR-0092 (the copy counter). An earlier draft of this ADR limited the tools to cookie-free ones; the author lifted that limit the same day.

## Context
streakboom.com is behind Cloudflare, on purpose. Cloudflare added a Web Analytics script, a bot-detection script and error-reporting headers by itself while the privacy page said "no analytics" (W-64). The review `docs/plans/website-measurement-review.md` found where "no analytics" came from: D7 of ADR-0034 (2026-11-09) was option A of a multiple choice in which the website session recommended A, with its own reason ("a script on the site conflicts with a product whose selling point is privacy"); the author chose A and does not remember a principle behind it. ADR-0051 and the sticker handoff then wrote "the app's rule holds on the site". The charter and the app's contract are about Peak. The author's view: a site that promotes a product should carry the usual tools, with Cloudflare in front for safety.

## Decision
1. **The app's contract is unchanged and is the app's only:** charter article 1, `AGENTS.md` rule 4, the five promises, `docs/privacy.md`, ADR-0009. Nothing here touches them.
2. **The website is not bound by the app's privacy contract.** It may carry measurement and tracking tools, **each approved by the author and listed here**. A tool not on the list is refused by the build and by the check of the live site. Approved today: Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools (nothing on the page); **Cloudflare Web Analytics**; the **copy counter** of ADR-0092. To add another (for example Google Analytics 4, Microsoft Clarity, an advertising pixel) the author names it, and the website session adds it to the list, to the build's allowed addresses, to the privacy words (three languages) and to this ADR.
3. **Cloudflare protects the site, not the visitor's data from a tool:** a tool such as Google Analytics sends data to its vendor whatever Cloudflare does. The privacy page names every tool and what it receives.
4. **A consent notice for visitors outside Vietnam and Japan** comes with the first tool that sets a cookie or an identifier, **before** that tool runs for those visitors: a first-party notice (no third-party consent script), the choice kept in the browser, the tool loaded only after "yes" for those visitors. How a visitor is placed (time zone and language, not a request about the person) and the exact words are decided when the first such tool is approved. Cloudflare Web Analytics and the copy counter set no cookie (Cloudflare says so, not verified), so no notice is built yet. The legal reading is a question for the lawyer (`docs/foundation/legal-questions.md`, 9).
5. **Cloudflare Web Analytics has three modes** in `scripts/site/catalog/measurement.json`: `off`; `auto` (Cloudflare adds its own script; the build adds nothing); `manual` (the build adds one script tag with the public site token). **The author chose `auto` with the dashboard option "Enable, excluding visitor data in the EU"** on 2026-11-14 (he asked why the automatic mode had to be switched off: it did not; the manual mode only lets the repository show the script, while the automatic mode has the EU exclusion built in and needs no token). With `excludeEu: true` the privacy words say visitors in the EU are not counted; that is a statement about the Cloudflare setting, which the build cannot see, so it is true only after the author has chosen that option. The live check allows exactly the script Cloudflare adds in `auto` mode and nothing else.
6. **Not wanted, to be switched off in Cloudflare:** the bot-detection script (JavaScript detections, Bot Fight Mode; the probable cause of the 403 on the download ledger, W-64) and Network Error Logging. They are not measurement the project asked for, and the first may decide who sees the page.
7. **The words follow the build.** The paragraph "This website" of the privacy page and the note of the sticker page are composed from the switches (`siteParagraph`, `stickerNote`) in three languages; the privacy page says that Cloudflare delivers the site. Adding a tool adds its sentence; the build refuses a tool without one.
8. **Two guards stay:** the build refuses any third-party script not on the list, a page without a listed script that is switched on, and a wrong token; `scripts/site/livecheck.mjs` reads the **live** pages after each deploy (Pages workflow) and fails on anything Cloudflare or anyone adds that the words do not say. A page Cloudflare turns away is reported as not checked.

## Consequences
- Until the author has chosen the dashboard option "Enable, excluding visitor data in the EU" the new words about the EU would be false: **do not deploy before that**. The live check cannot see the option; it sees the bot script and the error-reporting headers.
- Documents amended (pointers): ADR-0034 (D7), ADR-0051 (item 5), ADR-0052, ADR-0067, ADR-0092, `docs/website.md`, `docs/plans/website-seo-geo.md`, `spec/13-sticker-page.md` (technology row, section 6), the sticker plan. The handoff `website-stickers.md` is a received note and is not edited; this ADR says it no longer binds the site. A note for the app session (`docs/handoff/website-measurement-note.md`) covers the README and `docs/privacy.md`.
- The limits of the copy counter (no identifier, no cookie, no stored address, one event) are **that counter's own design** (ADR-0092), no longer a rule of the site; the author may widen them by a new decision.
- **Risks the author accepted:** a visitor who read the product's privacy promise may be surprised by trackers on the site (the privacy page separates the app from the site and names every tool); tracking with cookies or identifiers usually needs consent in the EU and has rules in Vietnam and Japan, which Cloudflare does not replace (not checked with a lawyer).
- **Not verified:** that Cloudflare Web Analytics sets no cookie or identifier; which Cloudflare setting produces each added item; the legal reading; the Vietnamese and Japanese words (AI-written, no native reader).
