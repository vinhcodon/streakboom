# ADR-0136: The flick is the shove: a strict rule that prefers missing a flick to firing on a pass

- **Status:** accepted on 2026-11-18 (the author chose to build IR-4b with the strict rule proposed from the scripted rounds). **Continues ADR-0135** (the fine sampler) and **ADR-0133** (the right click is step aside; the shove waits for the flick). A **new gesture** (`flick`), which is why it has a record. Built and tested in code (124 Rust tests, 416 JS tests, the scripted rounds); **the code is not committed until the author has looked at a test build** (rule 8); **in a Preview or developer build only** (the sampler starts only there).
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Decided by:** Vinh (author), recorded by Claude (the app session)

## Decision
1. **The flick is a gesture of the input map** (`flick`) and the shove is its action: Peak staggers **in the way it was flicked** (a flick to the right is a stagger to the right; near the end of a seat it teeters as the push always did). Its gates are the shove's: not quiet, not held, not cooling down (2.5 s, shared with the old push).
2. **The rule** (`fastpass.rs`, looked at after every sample of the fine sampler, on the last 120 ms of the ring): a **fast stroke** (a speed, read over spans of 24 ms or more, of at least **1500 px/s** somewhere in the window; a path of at least 60 px, mostly sideways), after which **the hand has stopped** (a speed over the last 24 ms or more of at most **250 px/s**) **on the sprite** (the last position within 15 px of Peak's square). It is looked for again only after 1.5 s. The speeds are those of the pointer **on the screen**, not relative to Peak, because Peak moves (it looks, steps aside, staggers) and the window with it. The stop is an **absolute** speed and not a share of the top speed: the first version (a mean speed of 1000 px/s, an end speed of at most half of the top speed) fired on 4 to 6 of 27 scripted crossings, because the time between two samples is not exact and a ratio of two noisy speeds swings. A pointer that passes Peak at speed has not stopped when it is on the sprite, so it does not fire. This means the hand must **rest on Peak for a moment** (about 24 ms, a few samples) after the strike, as a hand does when a strike is over.
3. **It errs on the side of missing.** In the last scripted run of three rounds it fired on **12 of 48 flicks and on none of the 81 other passes** (27 crossings, 12 runs along the taskbar, 24 rubs, 18 approaches). The charter (article 2: Peak does not take offence at a mouse that is only going somewhere) ranks a false shove above a missed one. The strength of the rule is **not a promise for a real hand**: it is read from a script and a small sample, and the first look of the author's own hand decides whether it is too strict.
4. **What goes to the page:** one event, `percept` with `kind: "flick"`, the **way** (left or right) and a **power** (1, or 2 for a mean speed of at least 2500 px/s). No position, no speed, no time. A test build writes one fixed line of numbers (`flick_fired way= power= vpeak= vend=`) for calibration. The page runs `resolveGesture("flick")` and then the same `shove` as before; the hint about "step aside" after repeated pushes (IM-4) counts flicks too.
5. **Where it runs:** the fine sampler starts only in a Preview or developer build (ADR-0135). **A Stable build does not have the flick**, and the right click remains step aside; whether and when it reaches Stable is a later decision after the author has used it for a while.

## Rejected
- **A looser rule** (the speed alone; or the speed and an end speed that is a share of the top speed): it fires on transits (the speed alone on 9 of 11 fast crossings and every run seen along the taskbar; the ratio on 5 or 6 of 27 crossings in the live run).
- **Judging the whole pass when the pointer leaves** (what the first numbers were made of): up to a quarter of a second late, and a pass that began with a slow approach is not a flick. The live rule looks at the last 120 ms.
- **A power that scales the stagger:** kept in the event for later; the shove has one strength for now.

## Consequences
`fastpass.rs` (the rule and its tests), `lib.rs` (the event and the trace line), `bindings.ts` (the gesture `flick`, the shove's gesture), the page (`onPercept` runs the shove), the privacy test (the page is told of nothing but the flick, with a way and a power), spec 17. The measurement scripts (`drive-pass.ps1` now rests 45 ms after a scripted flick) and `docs/plans/verification-debt.md` (V-171, V-172).
