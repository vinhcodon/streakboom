## 1. Flows (the desktop path is the main one)

Principles for every flow: the page does the copy **inside the click** (the blob is prepared before: the promise is handed to the clipboard item, the pattern the page already uses); the picture stays a real `<img>` so the browser's own menu (right-click Copy image, long press Share or Save) always works; nothing is a download button (ADR-0053: no per-sticker Download, no ZIP) and no flow needs an account.

### 1.1 Desktop (Windows first; Zalo PC and Teams desktop and web)
1. Open the hub or a permalink, type a few letters (or pick a chip) and **click a sticker: one click** puts the PNG on the clipboard; a short line in Peak's voice confirms.
2. In the chat box, **paste (Ctrl+V)**, then Enter. That is **two actions on the page side** (click, paste) and meets H1's "at most 2 clicks" if paste counts as the second.
3. Secondary: **drag the picture** into the chat window. Whether Zalo and Teams accept a dragged image from a browser (as an inline picture, as an attachment, or not at all) is **not known**: it is a cell of the test table. The picture must stay `draggable` (a real `<img>`).
4. Fallback: right-click the picture, Copy image (always works, from the browser).

### 1.2 Mobile (Android and iOS; Zalo and Teams apps)
Two choices for the main action on a touch device. **Answered (Q8): B, after a test on a phone;** until then A stays.
- **A. As today:** a tap copies, the confirmation carries a Share link (tap 1 copy, tap 2 Share, then pick the app in the sheet: three taps before the chat).
- **B. Share first:** on a touch device where `navigator.canShare({files})` is true, **a tap opens the share sheet at once** (tap 1, then pick the app); where it is false, a tap copies and the line says "paste it in the chat". A small, text-only "Copy" stays available in the sticker's permalink page. This needs a decision (it changes ADR-0051's "tap copies") and a real-device test (V2): until then A stays.

### 1.3 What is known about the chat apps (nothing is claimed beyond this)
- **Quick test of all platforms by Vinh, 2026-11-13 (a summary, no cell details):** Vinh tried Zalo and Microsoft Teams on all the platforms he has (the desktop app, the web and the phones) and found the result **OK**; Teams "will go into real use and issues will be fixed later". No cell of the matrix was filled, so what "OK" means per app (transparency, size, drag-and-drop, dark theme) is **not recorded and not verified here**.
- **Zalo, one device, not recorded which** (reported by Vinh, 2026-11-12): pasting or sharing the sticker worked; the picture looked **much larger than Zalo's own stickers**; **the background was white, not transparent**; at 180 px it was still large, at 128 px it was **too small and of low quality**, so the original 360 px PNG was restored (ADR-0075). The website session checked that the source files are transparent (RGBA) and that the picture put on the clipboard is still transparent (checked in a browser, not in Zalo); so the white background is probably added by the receiving side (the clipboard route of the operating system or the chat app flattening the picture). **Not proven.** The matrix tells where it happens (paste the same copy into Paint and into other apps).
- **Teams:** nothing is known. Not looked up for this spec.
- **Messenger, Telegram, WhatsApp:** nothing is known for still pictures. (The handoff's notes on animated stickers are about a later phase.)
- Because a sticker pasted as a **picture** is a message of its own kind, it will look different from a real sticker pack in every app; this is the price of D1 (no sticker packs). The test decides how bad it is per app.

### 1.4 Dark theme
A transparent sticker in a dark chat (Teams dark, Zalo dark) must stay readable: the set is drawn to read on light and dark backgrounds (a light outline), which is **checked in the matrix**, not assumed. If a chat flattens onto white, the dark theme does not matter there; if it keeps transparency, it does.

### 1.5 Variants of the picture
- **T**: the original transparent PNG, 360 px (the default).
- **W**: the same picture on a white square (the handoff's "white background variant"). Built **on the client with a canvas** when the person asks, never as a second file per sticker (a thousand stickers would double the weight). Offered only if the matrix shows an app where T looks wrong and W looks right; until then it is a test file Vinh makes by hand.
- **S**: a smaller size (`?px=NN`, kept as a test parameter). A size chooser for visitors is **not** proposed: ADR-0060 and ADR-0074 found that "small and sharp" cannot both be had when an app treats the picture as an ordinary image.

## 2. The data model (one source)

Thousands of stickers in several languages, added continuously by Vinh, so adding one must be a small, safe change (one place, no code).

**Today:** `set-v2/manifest.json` has, per sticker, an `id` (`01` to `31`, also the file name), `text`, `title`, `alt` and `reviewed` per language, plus the licence and font credits. The numbers of a set are not stable across sets (the recent list is therefore kept per set, ADR-0073).

**Proposed (v0): keep the manifest and add a sidecar `tags.json`** keyed by the sticker's stable slug, so the sticker session's folder is not restructured. **Proposed (v1, when there are about 100 or more): one folder per sticker** (`catalog/<slug>/meta.json` and the pictures), because one big file is a merge conflict between sessions and a poor review. The change is decided when the count makes it worth it.

```json
{
  "slug": { "en": "good-morning", "vi": "chao-buoi-sang", "ja": "ohayou" },
  "added": "2026-11-12",
  "status": "published",
  "text":  { "vi": "Chào buổi sáng!", "en": "Good morning!", "ja": "おはよう！" },
  "title": { "vi": "Chào buổi sáng", "en": "Good morning", "ja": "おはよう" },
  "alt":   { "vi": "...", "en": "...", "ja": "..." },
  "reviewed": { "vi": true, "en": false, "ja": false },
  "emotions":   ["sleepy", "warm"],
  "situations": ["morning", "start-of-day", "monday"],
  "synonyms":   { "vi": ["chào", "dậy chưa", "sáng rồi"], "en": ["hello", "wake up"], "ja": ["朝", "起きた"] },
  "edge": 0,
  "related": ["sweet-dreams", "sleepy"],
  "layers": null
}
```

Rules:
- **`slug`** is per language (the address of the permalink) and **never changes** once published (a retired sticker keeps its address and says it is retired). The stable key of a sticker is its **English slug**; the recent and favourite lists store it (not a number).
- **Tags are a controlled list** (`tags.json` has the vocabulary): an id, a name per language, a slug per language, and **one or two sentences of introduction per language** (a tag page without its own words is thin content, section 4). A tag used by fewer than six stickers does not get a page.
- **`emotions`** are the feeling (sleepy, proud, fed up, delighted), **`situations`** are the occasion (Monday morning, 11:30, Friday afternoon, deadline, meeting, thanks, birthday). A sticker has one to three of each.
- **Tags are given by the system, not by hand (Q9, answered):** a **keyword dictionary** (`tag-rules.json`: for each emotion, situation and cheekiness level, the words and phrases, per language, that point to it) is applied **at build time** to the sticker's caption, title and alt text, and fills `emotions`, `situations`, `synonyms` and `edge`. The result is **deterministic and tested** (a table of captions with the expected tags). A sticker whose words match nothing makes the build name it, so the dictionary is extended (no sticker is published without tags). The build also writes **`tags.proposed.md`**, a one-page list of every sticker with the tags it got, for Vinh to skim once (and after each new batch); a correction is a line in an `overrides` file that wins over the rules. No AI service is called by the build (a secret key, a cost, and results that change between builds were refused). The website session writes the first dictionary for the 31 stickers.
- **`edge`** (how cheeky): `0` gentle, `1` cheeky ("lầy"), `2` very cheeky ("lầy muốn tắt thở"). The names shown are a decision of the author per language. The charter still applies: a sticker is a **reply** (sorry, too much for me), never a need of Peak ("I miss you", hunger); the build refuses a text with the words the audit refuses (health claims, "lonely"...).
- **`synonyms`** are words a person would type for the feeling, per language, without needing diacritics.
- **`layers`** (optional, v1 or later): `{ "art": "...", "box": {x, y, w, h}, "align": "center", "maxLines": 2, "font": "..." }` for stickers whose caption is drawn on the client (section 8). Absent means the caption is part of the picture, as in set-v2.
- **Languages:** a language is added when it is requested (Q3); a sticker without a picture in a language is not shown on that language's pages (it falls back to the English picture only if the author says so).
- **Order and the "new" shelf** come from `added`.
- **Build checks (all new):** every slug unique and never removed; every tag known; every published sticker has its pictures, text, title, alt, at least one emotion or situation, and at least one synonym in each language it is shown in; the controlled vocabulary used only as listed; the texts pass the word checks; the review flag drives the note.

## 3. Search (client side)

**Goal:** typing "met roi" finds "mệt rồi"; a typo still finds it; it works for two thousand stickers without a server, and without slowing the pick.

**Normalisation (one function, used by the build and by the page, with a test table):** Unicode NFD, lower case, remove combining marks, `đ` to `d`, remove punctuation, collapse spaces. Japanese: NFKC, katakana folded to hiragana, and characters matched as **bigrams** (no spaces in the text). The same function builds the index and normalises the query, so they cannot drift.

**Index (built at build time, one file per language, loaded when the search box is first focused or on idle):** for each sticker, the normalised fields with weights: title 3, caption text 3, synonyms 2, emotion and situation names 2, tag names 1. Size budget: **at most 150 KB gzip per language for 2,000 stickers**; over it, the index is split by first letter. No search library: the matching below is about a hundred lines, which keeps "no dependency" (a library such as MiniSearch is the alternative if the own code grows).

**Matching:** the query is split into tokens; a sticker matches if **every token matches** a field word by **prefix**, or by **edit distance 1** for tokens of 4 to 7 characters and **2** for 8 or more (transpositions count as one). Results are ranked by the weights, then (v1) by HOT, then by `added`. An empty box shows the default shelf. The match is on normalised text, so tone marks never matter (ambiguity such as "ma" is solved by ranking; the matching words are highlighted so the person sees why).

**No results:** a line in Peak's voice and, if the person wants, **a button "tell Peak which sticker you wanted"** that sends the phrase **only when pressed** (section 6). Nothing typed is sent automatically.

**Tests (written first, in `scripts/site/`):** a table of at least 40 queries per language with the expected first results (without diacritics, with typos, with synonyms, with a tag name, in Japanese kana and kanji), the index size check, and the normalisation function against Vietnamese words with every tone mark combination.

## 4. Pages, URLs and search engines

| Page | Address | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hub | `/stickers/`, `/vi/stickers/`, `/ja/stickers/` (as now) | the search box, the shelves (v1), the first 60 stickers **in the static HTML** (the rest from the index), the chips of emotions and situations (links to tag pages), the licence line, the recent and favourite row. |
| Permalink | `/stickers/<slug>/`, `/vi/stickers/<slug-vi>/`, `/ja/stickers/<slug-ja>/` | the picture, the title, the caption, its tags (links), related stickers, the same copy and share behaviour, the licence line, structured data (`ImageObject` with licence and creator), `hreflang` to the other languages' permalink, `canonical` to itself, the sticker's own picture as `og:image` (360 px: a `summary` card). |
| Tag | `/stickers/tag/<slug>/` (per language) | a short hand-written introduction, the stickers with that tag, links to related tags. |
| Search | **no address**: it lives in the page. A `?q=` address, if one is ever made, is `noindex` and `canonical` to the hub. |

- **Every published sticker is in `sitemap.xml`** (with its image) and the `hreflang` alternates; the sitemap protocol allows 50,000 URLs per file, far above this.
- **The risk of thin and near-duplicate pages:** a thousand permalinks with a picture and one line may be judged low quality and may drag the good pages down (this is a general risk and a **hypothesis**, not a finding). Controls: unique words per sticker (title, caption, alt, a one-sentence description), links between related stickers and tags, **publish permalinks in stages** (the 31 first, then in batches), and **read the Search Console coverage after each stage** before the next. No page is made `noindex` to hide the problem.
- **Static HTML first:** every permalink and tag page is **fully rendered at build time**; the script only adds copy, share, search and shelves. A crawler never needs to run script to see a sticker or a tag.
- **The weight of the site:** thousands of 20 KB pictures per language, in the repository and in the published site. GitHub documents limits for Pages (a published site of at most 1 GB, a soft bandwidth limit per month; to be re-read before relying on them). At 3,000 stickers by 3 languages by 20 KB the pictures are about 180 MB: under, but the repository grows with every sticker. This is why layers (section 8) matter at scale.
- **Build time:** page generation of a few thousand pages by the existing Node build is a matter of seconds; the checks and tests grow with it (the budgets in `checks.mjs` are updated by the same ADR that adds the pages).

## 5. Peak on the page (R12, R13, R14, R16, R17)

Peak is a companion on this page, not a feature: it **never covers the grid, never takes a click meant for a sticker, never slows the pick.**
- Placement: sitting on **the top edge of the search box** (or in the header), not floating over the grid. On a phone width it is hidden or static.
- It uses the rig that the home page already uses (`site/peak.js`); it loads **after the first idle and after the first interaction**, adds no layout shift (fixed size), and stops moving while the person types in the box, when the page is hidden, and always with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Its voice: the line after a copy has several variants per language, chosen by the sticker or the hour (the person's own clock only); the "no results" line; one invitation to the app **once, after the fifth copy**, in the line (not a popup), with a permanent "no thanks" kept in the browser. Lines obey the charter (replies and play; no need, no guilt, no health claim).
- **Budget:** the Lighthouse results of the hub must stay at 100 (accessibility, best practices, SEO) and at 99 or more for performance, with a layout shift of 0.000 and a total blocking time under 50 ms, **with Peak running** (measured on a clean runner, as for the earlier pages).

## 6. Measurement (section 11 of the handoff)

**Decided (Q7, 2026-11-13): anonymous counting from v1, with a small Cloudflare Worker (option 2 below); v0 counts nothing.** The privacy text of the sticker pages and of the privacy page changes **first**. The paragraph that follows explains what the decision changes.

**The conflict that was to be decided.** Today the sticker page promises "nothing is sent or recorded anywhere when you copy or share" (ADR-0051, ADR-0053, ADR-0060; the page says so in three languages). Most of the metrics need an event sent when someone copies. So **the page cannot measure without changing its own promise**, and that is Vinh's decision (Q7). What can be measured under each choice:

| Metric (handoff section 11) | Without any counting (today's promise) | With the minimal anonymous counting proposed below |
|---|---|---|
| Time from entering the page to the first copy or share | **Not measurable.** Moderated tests only: 5 to 8 people, a screen recording or a stopwatch (the plan's v0). | An event on the first copy of a page view with the **time bucket** (under 3 s, 3 to 10, 10 to 30, over 30), no identifier. |
| Share of sessions with at least one share | Not measurable. | `view` events (one per page load) against `copy`/`share` events, both with coarse platform and language. This is a **page view** rate, not a session or person rate (there is no session id). |
| No-result rate and the phrases | Not measurable. | The rate: a counter of `no-result`. The phrases: **only when the person presses "tell Peak which sticker you wanted"**; no automatic logging of what is typed. |
| Return rate after 7 days | Not measurable. | **Not exactly measurable** without a persistent id. A flag set in the browser's local storage ("first visit more than 6 days ago") sent as a yes or no with `view` gives a rough share of returning page views. |
| Platform and browser mix; share success against fallback | Search Console and the browser statistics of GitHub give nothing. | Coarse buckets (Windows, macOS, Android, iOS; Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox) and an outcome (`copied`, `shared`, `refused`). |
| Conversion to the app | The **daily history of GitHub downloads already exists** (ADR-0053) but cannot say where a download came from. | A `download-click` event from the sticker pages only, against the daily downloads. A click is not a download and a download is not an install (the site never calls them installs). |
| Visibility in search (H4) | **Search Console**: impressions, clicks, queries and indexing per page, free, with no tracking of visitors. Bing Webmaster Tools the same. | the same |

**The minimal counting, if approved** (one event type per line, no identifier, no cookie, no IP stored, no per-person record; only **daily counters per sticker and per bucket**):
`view` {language, platform bucket, returning flag}, `copy` / `share` / `refused` {sticker slug, route, time bucket, first-in-view flag}, `no-result` {language}, `download-click`, `suggest` {phrase, only on the button}.

**Backends, from the least dependent** (the handoff asks for the lightest):
1. **None (proposed for v0).** HOT is replaced by an **editorial "popular" row** chosen by Vinh; the metrics above that need counting are read with moderated tests and Search Console. Nothing to build, the promise stays true.
2. **A small Cloudflare Worker with a counter store** (one file of about 40 lines, free tier, an address on `workers.dev`, so no DNS change; it adds one vendor; Cloudflare's edge sees an IP address while the request passes, our code does not store or read it; the privacy page must say so). The site **reads** nothing from it at run time: a **daily job** (the Pages workflow already runs daily) fetches the totals and writes `hot.json` at build time, so the tab **HOT is static** and the Worker only receives increments. Abuse: increments are rate-limited by Cloudflare's rules and HOT shows **ranks, never numbers**; a bot can still skew a rank, which is accepted for a shelf of stickers.
3. **A hosted privacy-friendly counter** (for example GoatCounter): counts without cookies, but it is a **third-party script or beacon**, which the site has promised not to have (the build refuses third-party scripts). Not proposed.

Legal reading of anonymous counters (is a notice needed?) is **not concluded here**: it belongs to the lawyer's list (`docs/foundation/legal-questions.md`).

## 8. Captions: baked into the picture, or drawn on the client (R9, V5, Q5)

- **Today:** the words are part of each picture, made by the sticker session with fonts under the SIL OFL (Be Vietnam Pro for Vietnamese and English, M PLUS Rounded 1c for Japanese). 31 stickers by 3 languages is 93 pictures: this scales badly to thousands by several languages (every sticker is redrawn per language) and bloats the repository.
- **Alternative:** one picture of Peak per sticker without words (`art`), the caption drawn **in the browser with a canvas** at copy time (so the clipboard gets a PNG with the words), with the font loaded from the site. Cheaper per language, but the **permalink, the Open Graph picture and Google Images need a captioned picture**, so the build would have to draw it too (a rasteriser in the build: a new dependency; the build has none today), or those pages show the picture without words.
- **What was checked for V5 (2026-11-13, Chrome, the repository's own font files, a canvas, not committed):** Vietnamese with stacked tone marks ("Ừ thì sao nào?", "Ờ… được thôi.", "Nghỉ đi, mệt quá rồi…"), Japanese ("おはよう！", "お誕生日おめでとう！") and English **render correctly**; fitting the width by shrinking the font works; a **long caption shrinks until it is unreadable at a small size** ("Đừng làm phiền, đang bận!" at 128 px), so layers need **a line-break rule (two lines) and a maximum length**. **Not checked:** Safari and Firefox canvas text, the loading of the font before the first draw, the outline on light and dark backgrounds in a chat, scripts other than these three, and how the exported PNG looks after an app re-encodes it.
- **Proposed (Q5): hand-placed words (as today) for v0**, because v0 has 31 stickers and the rest of the page is the larger gain; **decide layers at the start of v1** with a test of Safari and of the build-time picture, and only for **new languages** (a hundred stickers in a fourth language), never for the stickers that exist.

## 11. Constraints and how this spec meets them

- **v0 is still PNG only;** animated stickers are not in this spec.
- **No account, no user content.** The only things kept are lists in the visitor's own browser (recent, favourites, a "no thanks" to the app invitation).
- **Backend: none in v0;** from v1 one small counter service (Q7 approved; section 6, option 2).
- **Peak never covers the grid** and has a budget (section 5).
- **Not another chat tool.** The page lets a person take a sticker; it does not send, store or show messages, has no feed, no reactions, no sharing of the person's choices. **The three questions:** *can another assistant do it with a prompt?* no (it is a catalogue of pictures of Peak); *does it make Peak bond more?* a little, it brings the character into the person's own conversations; *is it lovable with the AI off?* yes, it has no AI at all.
- **The site's promises hold:** no cookies, no third-party script, no tracking of copy or share in v0, and from v1 only the listed anonymous counts, after the privacy text changes; CC BY 4.0 and the credits stay; no "Zalo sticker" or "Telegram sticker" claim (they are pictures, not packs); the English and Japanese words keep their "not read by a native speaker" note until the manifest says reviewed.

## 12. Not in this spec
Animated stickers; the "Nói đại đi" free-text matching and embeddings (R7: later, and a matching service would be a new dependency and a privacy question); a PWA and its offline cache (planned for v1 in the plan, detailed then); a store submission to Zalo or Telegram (the author's call; ADR-0051).
